# “Yes, and…”

*Accept the reality your partner offers, and add your own. Improv has no script — reality is built offer by offer.*

> “Yes, and…” is the mortar that binds the bricks of a scene.

## Two halves

- **Yes** — acceptance. You agree with your partner's reality. Without “yes” there's no shared world: everyone plays their own scene.
- **And** — contribution. You add something new and move the scene. Without “and” the world exists but stalls in place.

## One setup, three answers

Setup: “Grandpa, you left the car out in the rain again.”

**Block**
— Grandpa, you left the car out in the rain again.
— I'm not your grandpa. And I've never owned a car.
*Cancelled everything. Nothing to build on.*

**“Yes” without “and”**
— Grandpa, you left the car out in the rain again.
— Oh, sorry. I'll go get it now.
*Accepted, but the scene didn't move. The sneakiest mistake.*

**“Yes, and…”**
— Grandpa, you left the car out in the rain again.
— Yes, and I did it on purpose. I wanted the rain to wash off what was on it from that road.
*Accepted and added mystery. Off we go.*

There are always many right “yes, and…”s — **any honest contribution beats a block.**

## It's not “agree with everything”

> **The key nuance.** “Yes, and…” works on the level of the scene's reality, not the character's opinions. Heroes can argue and fight — all that matters is whether the player accepted the offered fact.

**Block**
— You broke mom's vase!
— I never touched any vase.
*The player denies the fact itself. Reality collapses.*

**“Yes, and…”**
— You broke mom's vase!
— Yes, and I'm glad! I hated it my whole life!
*Accepted the fact and added an old grudge. The character is angry, but the offer is accepted.*

See the difference? A quarrel isn't a block. **An argument between characters is fuel. Denying a fact is the brake.**

## Offers aren't only words

Accept everything your partner gives: a line, a gesture (handed you a heavy box — hold it as heavy), an emotion (walked in crying — something happened), the environment (“it's so cold in this basement” — now you're in a basement).

## Traps

- **“Yes, but…” — a polite block** — Catch yourself on “but”.
- **“Yes, and…” into chaos** — You pile on a heap of unrelated info. One good “and” beats five random ones.
- **Accepting out of politeness** — You agreed but didn't invest.
- **The literal formula** — “Yes, and…” is a mindset, not a stamp at the start of every line.

## Chaos vs one “and”

- *Setup.* — “Honey, we're out of coffee.”
- *✕ Chaos.* — “Yes, and I'm an alien, and there's a bomb in the basement, and I'm your clone from the future!” — five offers in one turn, nothing for the scene to grab.
- *✓ One “and”.* — “Yes, and I finished the last cup on purpose — we need to talk sober.” — one offer that develops what's already there.

## Exercises

- **[Solo] Yes-and journal** — To any everyday event, mentally answer “yes, and…”.
- **[Pair] Planning a party** — Every line with “Yes, and…”. The scene gains energy.
- **[Anti] Nothing but no** — Play a scene where both block. Feel how dead it is.
