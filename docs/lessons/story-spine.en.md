# Story Spine

*A story is about change: at the start the world is one way, at the end another. It holds together on a seven-step spine.*

> The spine is a compass, not rails. It hints where to pull the scene, but never dictates the lines.

## The spine

- **Once upon a time…** — A time, a place — a hero, with the when and where right away. Place and time are the base offers.
- **Every day…** — The routine. Often repeated twice for rhythm.
- **But one day…** — The routine breaks. The story begins here.
- **Because of that…** *(key step)* — A consequence. It repeats — the chain keeps building.
- **Until finally…** — The climax.
- **And ever since…** — A new, changed world. The ending can settle across a few “and then…”.

## Example

- *Once upon a time* — a barista named Gosha — present day, a tiny corner café.
- *Every day* — he drew the same little heart in the foam.
- *But one day* — a customer left a note: “draw something real”.
- *Because of that* — he started drawing scenes from his life on the cups.
- *Because of that* — people started talking about the café.
- *Until finally* — the owner gave him a whole wall to paint.
- *And ever since* — Gosha paints on canvases.

## The story engine

The middle isn't a list of events, it's a **chain of causes**. The links “every day”, “but one day”, “and then” only mark the start and the tail. The whole rise rests on three words: because of that.

**✕ just in a row:** The knight went into the forest. And then met a dragon. And then ate a sandwich.
Any event can be cut or reordered — nothing changes. That's a list, not a story.

**✓ each caused by the last:** A dragon burned the knight's village. Because of that he spent a year learning to walk on coals. And as a result he blew out the dragon's fire in one breath.
Cut one link — the whole chain breaks. That's a story.

> **Remember.** The links mark the start and the end. But the whole rise rests on three words — because of that.
